## What Cable Has That IPTV Doesn't {#cable-advantages}

Honest assessment — cable does have some advantages:

**Reliability during internet outages**
Cable TV (coaxial) works without internet. IPTV requires a working internet connection. This is rarely a concern with modern broadband, but it's worth noting.

**Local channel availability**
Some local broadcast channels (ABC, CBS, NBC, Fox affiliates) are available in STREAMB4, but coverage varies by region. If local news is critical, you can supplement IPTV with a free OTA antenna ($20–$40) to catch all local channels in HD.

**No setup required**
Traditional cable plugs straight into a TV. IPTV requires installing a player app and entering credentials — a one-time 5-minute process, but still more than cable.

## FAQ {#faq}

**Will I miss any channels if I switch?**
For most households, no. STREAMB4 covers all major US and international networks. The only potential gap is hyperlocal channels (city council meetings, local community TV). Supplement with a free OTA antenna if local channels are important.

**Is IPTV reliable enough to fully replace cable?**
With a stable internet connection (25+ Mbps, ethernet preferred), STREAMB4 is as reliable as cable for the vast majority of viewing. The service's anti-freeze technology routes around server issues automatically.

**What happens if my internet goes down?**
IPTV stops working without internet. This is the one scenario where cable has an advantage. If your internet is frequently unreliable, address that first — a stable connection is the foundation of any IPTV setup.

**Can I get STREAMB4 on the same TV as my cable box?**
Yes. You can run STREAMB4 on a Firestick or Smart TV app while your cable box is still connected. Use your TV input selector to switch between them during your trial period.
