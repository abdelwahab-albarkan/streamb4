## What Is 4K Ultra HD? {#what-is-4k}

4K Ultra HD (UHD) refers to a display resolution of 3,840 x 2,160 pixels — four times the pixel count of 1080p Full HD. The practical result is a noticeably sharper, more detailed image, particularly on screens 50 inches or larger.

For streaming purposes, there are two important distinctions:
- **True 4K** — the source content was filmed or mastered at 4K resolution
- **Upscaled 4K** — lower-resolution content scaled up by the device

## Internet Speed Requirements {#speed}

| Quality | Minimum Speed | Recommended |
|---|---|---|
| HD (720p) | 5 Mbps | 10 Mbps |
| Full HD (1080p) | 10 Mbps | 15 Mbps |
| 4K Ultra HD | 25 Mbps | 35 Mbps |
| 4K HDR (peak bitrate) | 35 Mbps | 50 Mbps |
| Multiple 4K streams | 50 Mbps per stream | 100 Mbps+ |

Speed is only part of the equation. **Stability** matters more than peak speed for live TV. A 25 Mbps fibre connection with consistent latency outperforms a 100 Mbps connection with packet loss. Always test your connection stability with a tool like Fast.com or PingPlotter before streaming live sports in 4K.

## HDR, Dolby Vision & HDR10+ {#hdr}

4K resolution is only half the visual upgrade — HDR (High Dynamic Range) is what makes colours pop and shadows deepen:

- **HDR10** — open standard, supported by all 4K devices and most streaming content
- **HDR10+** — dynamic metadata version, supported on Samsung TVs and NVIDIA Shield
- **Dolby Vision** — proprietary format with the highest colour accuracy; Apple TV 4K and Fire TV 4K Max
- **HLG (Hybrid Log-Gamma)** — used for live broadcast HDR (sports channels)

STREAMB4 supports HDR10 and Dolby Vision on compatible devices. When a 4K HDR stream is active, your TV's HDR indicator light should illuminate — if it doesn't, check the display settings on your streaming device.

## How to Enable 4K on Your Device {#enable-4k}

**Firestick 4K Max:**
Settings → Display & Sounds → Display → Video Resolution → 4K Ultra HD → Match Original Frame Rate → On.

**Apple TV 4K:**
Settings → Video & Audio → Resolution → 4K SDR or 4K HDR → Format → 4K Dolby Vision.

**NVIDIA Shield:**
Settings → Display & Sound → Resolution → 4K (2160p) → Dynamic Range → Auto.

**Android TV box:**
Settings → Display → Screen Resolution → 2160p (4K) → Refresh Rate → Auto.

In your IPTV player (TiviMate), make sure stream quality is not capped. Go to Settings → Player → Video Track → Auto or Best Available.

## Tips for Best 4K Quality {#tips}

1. **Use wired ethernet** — WiFi at 5GHz delivers fast speeds but can experience interference. Ethernet eliminates this.
2. **Check your HDMI cable** — 4K HDR requires HDMI 2.0 or higher. Older HDMI cables may carry 4K resolution but drop HDR metadata.
3. **Position your router strategically** — if WiFi is unavoidable, a WiFi 6 router with MU-MIMO dramatically reduces drop-outs.
4. **Close background apps** — on Firestick especially, other apps consuming RAM can reduce decoder performance.
5. **Enable Match Frame Rate** — this tells your TV to switch refresh rates to match the stream (24Hz for movies, 50/60Hz for live TV) rather than converting internally.

## Common 4K Problems & Fixes {#troubleshooting}

**4K not available in IPTV player**
Some player apps cap output resolution. In TiviMate, go to Settings → Player → Hardware Acceleration → On. In IPTV Smarters, select ExoPlayer as the video player.

**Buffering on 4K channels but not HD**
Your connection may reach 4K speed but not consistently. Try 1080p to verify STREAMB4 is working, then switch back to 4K. If 1080p is clean, the issue is connection bandwidth to your device. Ethernet will fix this.

**HDR not activating on 4K streams**
Check your HDMI cable first — HDMI 2.0 is required for 4K HDR. Then check your TV's HDMI Mode settings. On Samsung, enable "HDMI UHD Color" for the input port you're using. On LG, enable "HDMI Deep Color" in Settings → Picture → HDMI UHD Color.
